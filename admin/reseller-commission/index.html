Reseller Commission Management ​

Admin surfaces for the RESELLER pricing tier: the per-passenger rates, the finance queue, and paying partners out.

Permission-gated (Access Roles). All routes require the ADMIN role plus a fine permission: reads need reseller-commissions:view, setting the rates needs reseller-commissions:configure, and previewing or recording a payout needs reseller-commissions:payout. See Access Roles.

reseller-commissions:view-worklist is a narrower read. It opens the payout worklist screen — the partner list, the queue drawn under it, and the rate config behind that screen's button — and nothing else. It is accepted BESIDE :view on those routes, never instead of it, so granting it takes nothing away from an existing :view holder. Pair it with reseller-commissions:payout to build a role that finds who is owed money and records the transfer, without reading a partner's monthly statement, their whole commission list, or any payout receipt. The confirmed-unpaid rows stay readable on the queue routes the worklist ticks them on. The endpoint table marks which routes accept it.

⚠️ The read action is view, not read, on purpose. A reseller-commissions:read would have been conferred by the cross-resource *:read wildcard, so every look-but-do-not-touch role would have started seeing partner earnings on deploy day. Same reasoning as admin-users:view, and the reason the narrow code is spelled view-worklist rather than read-worklist.

⚠️ explicitOnly shapes the catalog, not the API. The catalog entry sets it and lists the concrete codes without a reseller-commissions:* row, so a role builder driven by the catalog offers no single click that hands over the disbursement action. It is not a server-side check: POST /admin/roles with {"permissions": ["reseller-commissions:*"]} is accepted (201), and that wildcard confers every action on the resource. Review roles by their stored codes, not by what the builder screen can produce.

Launch checklist ​

Neither item is code, and both block go-live.

  1. Set minPayoutAmountSAR on the active configuration row. The built-in default is 0, which pays out any amount — and Ahlaan absorbs the bank fee, so a trivial payout is an unrecoverable loss.
  2. Grant reseller-commissions:view to the finance staff who need these screens. On deploy day only * holders can see them. For staff who record transfers but have no business reading a partner's statement, grant reseller-commissions:view-worklist plus reseller-commissions:payout instead. Neither role is a built-in template — both are built by hand in the role builder, per environment.

What a reseller earns ​

A fixed SAR amount per seated passenger, per flight leg, on a custom booking that was priced at the RESELLER tier — nothing for infants. The partner's prices are silver prices; the commission is paid separately from the booking.

The earner is the organization when the booking has one, and the booking's user is recorded on every row either way. An organization-less partner holding the tier on their own user row earns on that row.

Per leg, at that leg's own cabin ​

There are four rates: an adult and a child amount for ECONOMY, and an adult and a child amount for BUSINESS. Each flight leg is priced at its own cabin's pair, because a booking has no single cabin — the class is stored per leg, and a round trip can legitimately fly out in economy and back in business.

  • A one-way booking earns one leg's worth.
  • A round trip earns two, one per leg, so an all-economy round trip earns double what the same passengers earn one way.

Worked example — 8 adults and 2 children, economy out and business back, at the built-in 5 / 5 and 8 / 8:

outbound (ECONOMY):  8 × 5 + 2 × 5  =  50 SAR
return   (BUSINESS): 8 × 8 + 2 × 8  =  80 SAR
                            estimate = 130 SAR

The cabin and the rates of both legs are frozen onto the commission row when it accrues, so a later rate change never reprices it.

⚠️ A leg whose stored cabin cannot be read is priced at ECONOMY, never refused, and the row records UNKNOWN for that leg so the fallback stays visible. An unreadable cabin must not stop a customer's booking being marked paid.

⚠️ A ROUND_TRIP booking with no stored return flight is broken data. It earns the outbound leg alone — not a silent zero, and not an invented second leg. Its return rate fields stay empty.

⚠️ Closing a booking is what pays the reseller. Nothing is withdrawable until COMPLETED, and on the current flow nothing reaches COMPLETED without an admin doing it. A booking whose money arrived and which nobody closed is a partner who is owed money and cannot be paid — which is exactly what the finance queue exists to show.

Short deliveries and late tickets ​

The confirmed figure prices the passengers whose tickets were delivered. Each un-ticketed seat is deducted at the adult amount, because ticketedPaxCount is a single total with no adult/child breakdown — the same convention the customer-side shortfall estimate takes. The row stores unticketedPaxCount, so the assumption is auditable rather than inferred.

⛔ The deduction is taken PER LEG. A passenger ticketed on the outbound leg but not the return one flew ONE leg, and the reseller earned the outbound commission on him. The row is reduced by unticketedOutboundPaxCount × amountPerAdultSAR + unticketedReturnPaxCount × returnAmountPerAdultSAR, not by the summed rate times a single count. On a one-way row returnAmountPerAdultSAR is null and the arithmetic is the outbound rate alone, exactly as before.

⛔ unticketedPaxCount and confirmedPaxCount are the LARGER leg, never the two per-leg columns added together. They derive from the booking's ticketedPaxCount, which is min(outbound, return). Outbound short 0 with return short 3 stores 3, not 5.

⚠️ A consequence: where the legs differ, confirmedAmountSAR no longer divides by confirmedPaxCount. The amount is priced per leg while the counts stay seat counts, so reconcile it against the four rate columns and the per-leg counts — never against the scalar count. This now holds for EVERY row on the table; it used to be true only of the late-top-up row described below.

⛔ On a round trip the deduction is taken across BOTH legs. A passenger who was never ticketed flew neither of them, so a missing seat costs the sum of the two legs' adult amounts. On the mixed round trip above that is 5 + 8 = 13 SAR per missing seat, and 3 missing seats reduce the 130 SAR estimate to 91.

⚠️ Within each cabin the adult and child amounts are equal by default — 5 / 5 and 8 / 8 — which makes the deduction exact. Set the adult and child amount of the same cabin to different values and a short delivery is still deducted at the adult amount, so the confirmed figure will not match an exact adult/child split. Keep them equal within a cabin unless that is accepted. The economy and business pairs differing from each other is fine and is the point of the split.

If the missing tickets are uploaded after the close, a second commission row (sequence 2 and up) is written automatically, straight into CONFIRMED, priced at the first row's frozen adult amounts across the same legs — so the top-up reverses the deduction exactly, even if the configured rates changed in between. Re-uploading the same count mints nothing: the trigger is the booking's ticketed count measured against the commission already recorded, not the booking's status.

⚠️ On a round-trip top-up row, the amount spans both legs while the count columns stay seat counts. So estimatedAmountSAR ÷ estimatedAdultCount on such a row is not the adult rate — reconcile it against amountPerAdultSAR plus returnAmountPerAdultSAR, and against the row's own commissionedOutboundPaxCount / commissionedReturnPaxCount.

The top-up restores each leg SEPARATELY, and only the leg that moved is paid. A booking whose return tickets finally arrive is topped up at returnAmountPerAdultSAR alone, because the outbound leg was never deducted for those seats. The scalar counts on that row (estimatedPaxCount, confirmedPaxCount, commissionedPaxCount) carry the larger of the two restored figures, so a row that recovered 3 outbound and 0 return reads 3 rather than 0 — a row asserting zero passengers beside a positive amount would read as broken to anything multiplying a count by a rate.

⚠️ A booking closed through the shortfall route cannot reach that top-up. Recording the settlement pins the ticketed counts, so a later upload raising either leg is refused before any commission logic runs — measured on preprod 2026-08-14 against ROUND_TRIP booking B2U9GVS, settled at outbound 8 / return 7 of 10:

⛔ The pin is per leg, so it also catches a restatement that moves a leg while leaving the derived ticketedPaxCount untouched — outbound 8 / return 7 sent back as outbound 7 / return 8 is refused too, with a clause of its own.

Three statuses declare an edge to COMPLETED: TICKET_UPLOADED_FULL, SHORTFALL_REFUNDED and the legacy TICKET_RECEIVED. A booking closed from TICKET_UPLOADED_FULL is already at the seated total, so a re-upload leaves nothing to top up; the shortfall route is pinned as above. The top-up therefore applies to legacy TICKET_RECEIVED rows, not to bookings created on the current status ladder — the commission side is written to handle it, and the ticket side is what withholds it.

Note the two splits.

Read versus write. Reading a payout, including its transfer receipt, needs only reseller-commissions:view — looking at a payout does not require the right to record one. Moving money — previewing and recording — needs reseller-commissions:payout.

Worklist versus detail. reseller-commissions:view-worklist opens the view+ rows above and no others. So a role holding view-worklist + payout can list who is owed money and record the transfer — but totals, statement, the commission list, the payout list, the payout detail and the three proof routes all answer 403 for it, because each of those is gated on :view alone. That is the role the worklist screen needs; it is not a role that can audit a payout after the fact.

⚠️ view-worklist is not a privacy boundary. GET partners carries lastPaidPayout, which includes the destination bank code, bank name, account number and account name. A holder of the narrow code therefore reads a partner's bank account, even though the account is not a column on the worklist screen. What it withholds is the monthly statement, the whole commission list — paid and voided rows, and which payout claimed each one — the payout receipt file, and the maker/checker/payer trail resolved to colleague names. The confirmed-unpaid rows a payout is built from stay readable on the widened queue routes; the two paged ones filter by userId and organizationId.

⛔ That one permission is now the whole access decision. With no approval step, whoever holds reseller-commissions:payout can record a payout to an account they typed, or to no account at all, with nobody else in the loop and no route to reverse it. The compensating control is detective: the payout service writes its own audit rows, and a global interceptor copies every admin non-GET response into the audit table.

One route in this family takes no authentication at all: the no-login proof link, GET /api/v1/reseller-commissions/payout-proof/{token}. It is described under Inspect a payout, with the warning it needs.

Which earner key to send ​

organizationId and userId are not two spellings of the same filter, and the screen should not be deciding between them. Take the key from earnerScope on the payout worklist — the mapping is in earnerScope — which key to expand with. An organization-less partner is exactly the case that forces the question, and it is the case earnerScope answers: that row comes back USER, and userId is the only key that reaches its rows.

⛔ The earner keys AND, they do not OR. Sending both narrows to rows matching both, so an organizationId paired with a userId from outside that organization returns nothing rather than the union. Preprod, 2026-08-18, over 19 commission rows:

Querymeta.total

userId=<a member of that org>9

Rows two to four agree because that organization has one earning member in this data — on an organization whose members have each booked, the pair narrows to that member's bookings. The last row is why the pair is not a convenience: neither earner key is checked for existence, so an id that matches nothing is a 200 with an empty page, not a 404, and a screen that sends both keys "to be safe" shows an empty table instead of the partner's money.

⚠️ Send an earner key only when it carries a value. ?organizationId= with an empty value is read as no filter and answered the full 19 rows above — the same silence a misspelled filter gets. A form bound straight to the query string must omit the key rather than blank it.

Response: { "data": [...], "meta": { "total", "page", "limit", "totalPages" } }. Each row carries both stages (estimated and confirmed), the frozen per-passenger amounts and cabin for each flight leg, the earner, pricingTierAtBooking as the evidence the booking was priced as a reseller, the booking it was earned on, and payoutId / paidAt once it has been claimed and transferred.

displayStatus — the chip to print ​

⛔ PAID is not a stored status. status is ESTIMATED | CONFIRMED | VOID; a paid row still reads CONFIRMED and carries payoutId plus paidAt. That is deliberate — it keeps "confirmed but unpaid" a single predicate — so the server sends a ready-made label beside the stored value rather than a fourth enum member. status is unchanged and still means what it always did.

⚠️ IN_PAYOUT is no longer reachable for newly claimed rows. It requires payoutId set with paidAt still null, and recording a payout now sets both in the same transaction — so a row claimed today goes straight from AVAILABLE to PAID. Only rows claimed under the old request-then-approve flow can still show it.

Keep rendering the chip rather than computing the state from status: the value is still in the enum, and an environment that carries legacy rows will still emit it. But a screen should not expect to see it appear as a result of anything an admin does now.

payout is null on an unclaimed row and carries the id and status on a claimed one, so a committed row can link to the payout holding it. A live row points at a PAID payout, or — on a row claimed under the old flow — at a REQUESTED or APPROVED one. It never points at a REJECTED or CANCELLED payout, because closing a payout released its rows, so no chip is needed for those two.

legs — which way each leg actually runs ​

⛔ A booking stores ONE origin/destination pair. Drawing it on both legs is why a round trip renders as DMX → MKX twice. The return leg runs the opposite direction — destination → origin — and legs is that already worked out, so a screen does not have to know the rule.

  • ONE_WAY gets one entry. RETURN is absent, not null.
  • A ROUND_TRIP with no returnDate still gets two entries, the second with "date": null. The leg exists and only its date is missing; dropping the entry would draw the trip as one-way.
  • originName / destinationName can be null — the code always ships, the readable name is optional in the database. The block never substitutes the code for a missing name.
  • The flat origin / destination fields above are unchanged. legs is additive beside them, so nothing reading them today has to move.

⛔ The customer is NOT the commission's user. The accrual records the booking's own user as the earner, so on a reseller booking that relation IS the reseller — rendering it as "customer" prints the partner's own account on every row. contact is the traveller-side fact.

Two different dates. customBooking.createdAt is when the reseller made the booking; the commission row's own createdAt is when the customer's money arrived and the estimate was written. They differ by however long the invoice waited to be paid, so a screen with one "Tanggal" column has to pick deliberately. Both ship.

contact can be null. contactPerson is a JSON column, so its shape is a convention rather than a guarantee, and a row that is not an object with string email and phone reads as null rather than failing the request.

⛔ No traveller names and no prices, by design. passengers is unbounded and holds full PII, so the counts answer "how many" without shipping the manifest — open the booking for names. Every selling figure is gated by custom-bookings:read-pricing, and that stripping is applied by the custom-booking and invoice controllers rather than by this module, so a price here would reach a reseller-commissions:view holder unfiltered.

The per-leg fields on a commission row ​

FieldMeaning
amountPerAdultSAR / amountPerChildSARThe OUTBOUND leg's frozen rates
returnAmountPerAdultSAR / returnAmountPerChildSARThe RETURN leg's frozen rates. Empty on a one-way booking
outboundTripClass / returnTripClassThe cabin each leg was priced at: ECONOMY, BUSINESS, or UNKNOWN

Reading the empty values matters, because they mean different things:

  • returnTripClass and the return amounts empty → this booking has no return leg, or its stored return flight was missing.
  • outboundTripClass empty → the row was written before per-leg pricing shipped. Such a row was priced once for the whole booking, and its amountPer*SAR fields are that single figure.
  • either class set to UNKNOWN → that leg exists, its stored cabin could not be read, and it was priced at ECONOMY.

legs — the stored total, split across the legs it was priced on ​

The rate and cabin columns above are the raw material. legs is the answer: what the outbound leg earned, what the return leg earned, and how a shortfall was charged to each. It is derived on read — no column stores it, no stored total moves.

jsonc
"legs": {
  "outbound": {
    "tripClass": "ECONOMY",             // as PRICED; see the empty values above
    "amountPerAdultSAR": "5",
    "amountPerChildSAR": "5",
    "estimatedAmountSAR": "50",
    "unticketedOutboundPaxCount": 1,    // null until the booking is closed
    "commissionedOutboundPaxCount": 9,  // null until the booking is closed

  "return": null                        // see below for when this is null

⛔ The two counts inside legs are what the split was PRICED on, which is not always the stored column of the same name. On the commission list the stored row is returned alongside this block, so both appear on one object — and on a row confirmed before the per-leg columns existed the stored unticketedOutboundPaxCount is null while legs.outbound.unticketedOutboundPaxCount carries the booking-level unticketedPaxCount it fell back to. They are not in conflict: the top-level pair is what the row stores, and the pair inside legs is what the money beside it was computed from. Use the one inside legs to explain a leg's amount, and the stored pair to audit the row. The payout detail names its fields rather than returning the stored row, so only the legs copy appears there.

⛔ The two halves always add up to the figure beside them — to estimatedAmountSAR before the booking closes, and to confirmedAmountSAR after. If they would not, or if a half would come out negative, the whole legs block is null instead. It is never adjusted to make the sum work, so a client can add the halves and trust the answer, or find null and fall back to the single total.

⛔ The counts keep their full column names — unticketedOutboundPaxCount, not unticketedPaxCount. The short name is already on the same response object with a different meaning: the larger leg's figure, which is not the two per-leg counts added together.

legs.return is null whenever the row carries no return-leg rate, which the schema records as three correct readings of the same absence: a one-way booking, a row written before per-leg pricing, and a ROUND_TRIP whose stored return flight could not be read.

⚠️ customBooking.legs and legs can legitimately disagree. A ROUND_TRIP whose return flight was unreadable shows two entries in customBooking.legs — the trip really is a round trip — while legs.return is null, because that leg was priced at nothing. A return leg drawn with no money beside it is the honest rendering of such a row, not a bug.

⚠️ The count fields are null on an ESTIMATED row, not 0. Nothing has checked the tickets yet, and 0 would claim a check that found nobody missing.

Reserved fields — always empty ​

A commission row also carries unticketedAdultCount, unticketedChildCount, commissionedAdultCount and commissionedChildCount. Nothing writes them and nothing reads them. They are always null, and they are on the response only because the list query returns the whole row.

4. Pay a partner out ​

One call, one finance admin. The transfer is made at the bank first; this route records it. There is no request step, no approval step and no separate pay step — the payout is written PAID and the same instant is stamped on every commission row it claimed, because a paid payout whose rows still read as unpaid would let the same money be claimed twice.

⛔ There is no way back. No route reverses a PAID payout, and none ever did. What changed is that a payout now reaches PAID in a single call, so there is no intermediate state in which a mistake can be caught by somebody else. Preview first.

⚠️ Behaviour change: four routes are gone.

Every 400 above is raised before the proof is uploaded. Only the 409 can fire after it, and that path deletes the stored object and retires its File row.

A partly-resolving commissionIds list is refused outright rather than paid in part: at least one row is already claimed, already paid, still an estimate, void, or somebody else's, and paying a subset of what was asked for is worse than paying nothing.

⚠️ commissionIds: [] is a 400. It used to mean the same thing as omitting the field — claim EVERYTHING — so a screen posting its selection array unconditionally paid out the partner's whole balance the moment nothing was ticked. "Nothing selected" and "take it all" must not be spelled the same way. Omitting the field still means everything available.

Duplicated ids are also a 400, naming the field. They previously surfaced as a 409 about rows nobody else had claimed.

If no rate is published and none is sent, the request is refused rather than recorded at a rate of 1 — an IDR payout at 1 would claim the partner was sent one rupiah per riyal owed.

Two guards, and they catch different things ​

The window is an order-of-magnitude check and always blocks. USD accepts 2–8 and IDR accepts 200–20000 by default, both configurable per environment. It exists because an inverted rate is an ordinary-looking number: a 0.00023529 typed for IDR, or a 0.2667 typed for USD, sits unchallenged inside any single global bound wide enough to be useful.

The reconcile is an accuracy check on top, and it does not block. Now that a payout is recorded after the money has left the bank, a leg that fails to back-convert is a bank fee or a rounding decision rather than grounds to refuse the record of it. payoutAmount and payoutRate are converted to SAR through the row's own direction and compared against the SAR owed with a 1% tolerance; a divergence is stored and comes back as payoutReconciled: false:

⚠️ A PDF receipt downloads rather than previewing. Only bytes that prove to be a PNG, JPEG or WebP are served inline; everything else, PDF included, is forced to a download. The type is decided by the file bytes: the type recorded at upload is kept only when a signature confirms it, and replaced when the bytes contradict it.

{ "revoked": 0 } is a success, not an error. It also does not mean the file is unreachable — a token minted for the same file through the generic POST /storage/{fileId}/token is a different rail and is not deleted here. Verified on preprod: after this revoke returned { "revoked": 1 }, a generic token for the same receipt still served it. That rail retires a token on its own terms — POST /storage/{fileId}/token defaults maxDownloads to 1, so it is spent after one download — or through DELETE /storage/tokens/{tokenId}, which takes the token row's id and not the token string.

All three answer 404 for a payout that does not exist, a payout carrying no receipt, and a receipt whose stored file is gone — one answer for all three, so a caller cannot tell which.

5c. Building the Reseller Payouts screen ​

Everything the screen needs, in the order it is needed. Field meanings live in the worklist above; this section is the wiring.

The whole screen is one call ​

⛔ Do not sum the visible rows to fill a card. summary covers every partner and meta covers the page; on page 2 they disagree, and that is correct. The same rule holds one level down — the per-reseller footer "15 transaksi confirmed belum dibayar · SAR 1,018" comes from that reseller's payableRowCount and availableSAR on the card, not from adding up the expanded table, which is itself only the first page of that partner's history.

The dropdown is one parameter and nothing else has to change; the mapping from its three Indonesian labels is in the hasUnpaid row of the worklist query table.

Expanding a reseller ​

⛔ The two scopes are not interchangeable. Sending ?userId= for an ORGANIZATION earner narrows to one member's bookings rather than to the partner, and the table will silently show less money than the card promises. Branch on earnerScope, never on "does this row have an organization". The same branch feeds the payout history — see earnerScope — which key to expand with.

⚠️ Send no unpaid parameter here. The expanded table is the partner's whole history — that is why PAID and VOID rows appear in it. The payable subset is the footer, and it comes from the card.

⚠️ Tanggal is the booking's createdAt, not the commission's. The commission row's own createdAt is when the customer's money arrived, which is a different day whenever the invoice waited to be paid. A single date column has to pick one deliberately.

The Status chip has five values, not four ​

⛔ Print displayStatus, never the stored status. The server already derives this; re-deriving it in the client is how the two drift. The stored enum has three values and a paid row still reads CONFIRMED on it.

⚠️ IN_PAYOUT is the one the mockup is missing, and it is now rare rather than gone. A payout is written PAID with paidAt stamped on its rows in the same transaction, so a freshly recorded row goes straight from AVAILABLE to PAID. IN_PAYOUT still describes rows claimed by a payout written under the old flow — CONFIRMED, no paidAt, but not free to claim again — so a table rendered from four chips draws one of those exactly like a payable row and the footer then reads lower than the rows appear to justify. Give it its own chip and link it to the payout with payout.id.

Record Payout, all the way to the money landing ​

⛔ The transfer happens first, and outside this system. Nothing here talks to a bank. The route records a transfer that has already been made, which is why it requires a reference and refuses a blank one, and why a leg that fails to reconcile is stored rather than rejected.

⛔ Preview is the only checkpoint. There is no intermediate state and no second person, so the figure shown by payouts/preview is the last chance to catch a mistake before the payout is written. It is advisory — nothing is reserved, and a concurrent payout can still claim the rows in between, which answers 409.

⛔ The two money columns do not add up to anything ​

earnedConfirmedSAR is what became payable during the month. paidSAR is what was transferred during it. Commission confirmed in August and transferred in September sits in August's earned column and September's paid column — the same money, a month apart, in both.

closingCarrySAR is the bridge: confirmed and still unpaid at the month end, equal to the previous month's carry plus this month's earned minus this month's paid, rolled forward from openingCarrySAR.

Do not build a "total earned" by summing the two columns, and do not reconcile one month's paidSAR against the same month's earnedConfirmedSAR — a payout routinely claims rows confirmed across several months, and confirmedUpTo makes that the normal case rather than the exception.

VOID rows are excluded from every figure ​

A cancellation writes status, voidedAt and voidedReason and leaves confirmedAt and confirmedAmountSAR on the row. So a booking confirmed in August and cancelled in September still reads as August money from its dates alone, and a figure selected on a date range without a status test would count it. Every figure here carries status = CONFIRMED, which is why each month publishes that status beside the date bounds that reproduce it.

In practice a month a partner has already read does not shrink underneath them, because a booking can only be cancelled while it is still open and commission is only confirmed when it closes. The status test is what makes that a guarantee rather than an ordering accident, which is why it is on every predicate.

There is no estimate column ​

There is no estimatedAt column on the model, so a monthly estimate would have to be grouped by createdAt — and a late-ticket top-up row, written with its estimate leg and its confirmed leg equal at one instant, would land in both columns of one month. An estimate can also still fall when the ticket source under-delivers. The live figure stays on /totals, which is a screen rather than a document.

Months are cut in the app clock ​

timezone is returned rather than assumed. A commission confirmed at 23:30 UTC on the last day of a month belongs to the NEXT month here, because that is 02:30 the following morning in Asia/Riyadh. Render periodStart and periodEnd in that zone, not the browser's. periodEnd is exclusive — the first instant of the next month.
