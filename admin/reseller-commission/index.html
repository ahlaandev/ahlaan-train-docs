Reseller Commission Management ​

Admin surfaces for the RESELLER pricing tier: the per-passenger rates, the finance queue, and paying partners out.

Permission-gated (Access Roles). All routes require the ADMIN role plus a fine permission: reads need reseller-commissions:view, setting the rates needs reseller-commissions:configure, and starting, approving, rejecting or paying a payout needs reseller-commissions:payout. See Access Roles.

⚠️ The read action is view, not read, on purpose. A reseller-commissions:read would have been conferred by the cross-resource *:read wildcard, so every look-but-do-not-touch role would have started seeing partner earnings on deploy day. Same reasoning as admin-users:view.

⚠️ explicitOnly shapes the catalog, not the API. The catalog entry sets it and lists the three codes without a reseller-commissions:* row, so a role builder driven by the catalog offers no single click that hands over the disbursement action. It is not a server-side check: POST /admin/roles with {"permissions": ["reseller-commissions:*"]} is accepted (201), and that wildcard confers all three actions. Review roles by their stored codes, not by what the builder screen can produce.

Launch checklist ​

Neither item is code, and both block go-live.

  1. Set minPayoutAmountSAR on the active configuration row. The built-in default is 0, which pays out any amount — and Ahlaan absorbs the bank fee, so a trivial payout is an unrecoverable loss.
  2. Grant reseller-commissions:view to the finance staff who need these screens. On deploy day only * holders can see them.

What a reseller earns ​

A fixed SAR amount per seated passenger, per flight leg, on a custom booking that was priced at the RESELLER tier — nothing for infants. The partner's prices are silver prices; the commission is paid separately from the booking.

The earner is the organization when the booking has one, and the booking's user is recorded on every row either way. An organization-less partner holding the tier on their own user row earns on that row.

Per leg, at that leg's own cabin ​

There are four rates: an adult and a child amount for ECONOMY, and an adult and a child amount for BUSINESS. Each flight leg is priced at its own cabin's pair, because a booking has no single cabin — the class is stored per leg, and a round trip can legitimately fly out in economy and back in business.

  • A one-way booking earns one leg's worth.
  • A round trip earns two, one per leg, so an all-economy round trip earns double what the same passengers earn one way.

Worked example — 8 adults and 2 children, economy out and business back, at the built-in 5 / 5 and 8 / 8:

outbound (ECONOMY):  8 × 5 + 2 × 5  =  50 SAR
return   (BUSINESS): 8 × 8 + 2 × 8  =  80 SAR
                            estimate = 130 SAR

The cabin and the rates of both legs are frozen onto the commission row when it accrues, so a later rate change never reprices it.

⚠️ A leg whose stored cabin cannot be read is priced at ECONOMY, never refused, and the row records UNKNOWN for that leg so the fallback stays visible. An unreadable cabin must not stop a customer's booking being marked paid.

⚠️ A ROUND_TRIP booking with no stored return flight is broken data. It earns the outbound leg alone — not a silent zero, and not an invented second leg. Its return rate fields stay empty.

The two stages, and which admin act drives each ​

StageTriggerWritten by
ESTIMATEDThe customer's invoice is marked PAIDThe payment settlement path — the gateway webhook, an approved manual transfer, or an admin marking the invoice paid
CONFIRMEDThe booking is closed (COMPLETED)Whoever closes it. On the current flow that is a manual admin act on PATCH /admin/custom-booking/:id/status
VOIDThe booking is cancelled before its commission was claimed into a payoutThe same status route

⚠️ Closing a booking is what pays the reseller. Nothing is withdrawable until COMPLETED, and on the current flow nothing reaches COMPLETED without an admin doing it. A booking whose money arrived and which nobody closed is a partner who is owed money and cannot be paid — which is exactly what the finance queue exists to show.

Short deliveries and late tickets ​

The confirmed figure prices the passengers whose tickets were delivered. Each un-ticketed seat is deducted at the adult amount, because ticketedPaxCount is a single total with no adult/child breakdown — the same convention the customer-side shortfall estimate takes. The row stores unticketedPaxCount, so the assumption is auditable rather than inferred.

⛔ On a round trip the deduction is taken across BOTH legs. A passenger who was never ticketed flew neither of them, so a missing seat costs the sum of the two legs' adult amounts. On the mixed round trip above that is 5 + 8 = 13 SAR per missing seat, and 3 missing seats reduce the 130 SAR estimate to 91.

⚠️ Within each cabin the adult and child amounts are equal by default — 5 / 5 and 8 / 8 — which makes the deduction exact. Set the adult and child amount of the same cabin to different values and a short delivery is still deducted at the adult amount, so the confirmed figure will not match an exact adult/child split. Keep them equal within a cabin unless that is accepted. The economy and business pairs differing from each other is fine and is the point of the split.

If the missing tickets are uploaded after the close, a second commission row (sequence 2 and up) is written automatically, straight into CONFIRMED, priced at the first row's frozen adult amounts across the same legs — so the top-up reverses the deduction exactly, even if the configured rates changed in between. Re-uploading the same count mints nothing: the trigger is the booking's ticketed count measured against the commission already recorded, not the booking's status.

⚠️ On a round-trip top-up row, the amount spans both legs while the count columns stay seat counts. So estimatedAmountSAR ÷ estimatedAdultCount on such a row is not the adult rate — reconcile it against amountPerAdultSAR plus returnAmountPerAdultSAR instead.

⚠️ A booking closed through the shortfall route cannot reach that top-up. Recording the settlement pins ticketedPaxCount, so a later upload raising it is refused before any commission logic runs — measured on preprod against booking RKHJMRA, closed at 8 of 10:

POST /api/v1/admin/custom-booking/<id>/ticket   (ticketedPaxCount=10)
400 Bad Request
"Booking RKHJMRA has 8 passenger(s) ticketed and its shortfall has already been
 settled; the money for the remaining 2 has gone back to the customer. ...
 Raising the count to 10 would give them the tickets as well as the refund."

Three statuses declare an edge to COMPLETED: TICKET_UPLOADED_FULL, SHORTFALL_REFUNDED and the legacy TICKET_RECEIVED. A booking closed from TICKET_UPLOADED_FULL is already at the seated total, so a re-upload leaves nothing to top up; the shortfall route is pinned as above. The top-up therefore applies to legacy TICKET_RECEIVED rows, not to bookings created on the current status ladder — the commission side is written to handle it, and the ticket side is what withholds it.

This replaces the active row rather than patching it. The previous row is deactivated and kept as history, and there is no update route: a commission row keeps a foreign key to the exact configuration it was priced from, so rewriting a row in place would retroactively change what a partner was told they earned.

⚠️ The two business amounts are the exception to "send everything". They are optional so that a request body written before those fields existed keeps working. An omitted business amount is stored as the matching economy amount — not as 0, and not as "unchanged".

⛔ So a forgotten business amount is a wrong rate, not an error. Nothing on screen looks broken; the figure is simply the economy one. Read the configuration back after setting it and check businessAdultAmountSAR is what you meant before calling the environment done. An explicit 0 is kept as 0.

A rate change never reprices commission already earned. The amounts are frozen onto each commission row when it accrues, so a change applies only to bookings paid after it.

GET /history returns every row ever set, newest first, limit capped at 100. GET /defaults returns the built-in amounts alone.

bash

Each row carries both stages (estimated and confirmed), the frozen per-passenger amounts and cabin for each flight leg, the earner, pricingTierAtBooking as the evidence the booking was priced as a reseller, the booking it was earned on, and payoutId / paidAt once it has been claimed and transferred.

The per-leg fields on a commission row ​

FieldMeaning
amountPerAdultSAR / amountPerChildSARThe OUTBOUND leg's frozen rates
returnAmountPerAdultSAR / returnAmountPerChildSARThe RETURN leg's frozen rates. Empty on a one-way booking
outboundTripClass / returnTripClassThe cabin each leg was priced at: ECONOMY, BUSINESS, or UNKNOWN

Reading the empty values matters, because they mean different things:

  • returnTripClass and the return amounts empty → this booking has no return leg, or its stored return flight was missing.
  • outboundTripClass empty → the row was written before per-leg pricing shipped. Such a row was priced once for the whole booking, and its amountPer*SAR fields are that single figure.
  • either class set to UNKNOWN → that leg exists, its stored cabin could not be read, and it was priced at ECONOMY.

Reserved fields — always empty ​

A commission row also carries unticketedAdultCount, unticketedChildCount, commissionedAdultCount and commissionedChildCount. Nothing writes them and nothing reads them. They are always null, and they are on the response only because the list query returns the whole row.

They are reserved for a possible future change that would price a short delivery per passenger type instead of at the adult amount. That change is not built. No endpoint accepts or returns an adult/child breakdown of a ticketed count, the ticket upload still takes one total, and every shortfall figure — the partner's deduction and the customer's refund alike — is still computed at the ADULT amount.

⛔ Do not build against these fields. They have no writer, so they will read null on every row until the change that populates them ships.

3. The finance queue ​

bash
curl -X GET '/api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/queue?limit=50' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <admin-token>'

Two lists, and the second is the point.

ListWhat it holdsAction
closedUnpaidConfirmed commission with no payout against it, oldest confirmation firstPay it out
moneyReceivedNotClosedBookings that carry a commission row and are not closed yet, earliest departure firstClose the booking, or find out why it is stuck

limit defaults to 50 and is capped at 100, and applies to each list.

moneyReceivedNotClosed is the mitigation for a manual close. Since only a human closes a booking, and closing is what makes commission payable, this is the only screen on which "a partner is owed money and nobody has acted" is visible.

4. Pay a partner out ​

Both a reseller's own request and an admin batch create the same REQUESTED payout, through the same method — so the payout minimum and the claim guard bind both. A batch that skipped either would be a silent way around the rule.

REQUESTED --approve--> APPROVED --pay--> PAID
    |                     |
    +-------reject--------+---> REJECTED   (commission released back)
    |
    +--cancel (reseller)------> CANCELLED  (commission released back)

Start a batch payout ​

bash
curl -X POST '/api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/payouts' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <admin-token>' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{
    "organizationId": "<orgId>",
    "userId": "<partner-user-id>",
    "destinationAccountNumber": "1234567890",
    "destinationAccountName": "PT Mitra Reseller"
  }'
FieldTypeRequiredNotes
userIdstringyesThe partner user on the commission rows. An organization-less partner has no organization to be identified by, and the payout records a user either way
organizationIdstringnoOmit for an organization-less partner
commissionIdsstring[]noSpecific rows to pay. Omit to claim everything confirmed and unpaid for this earner
destinationBankCodestringnoMax 50 chars
destinationBankNamestringnoMax 120 chars
destinationAccountNumberstringnoMax 50 chars
destinationAccountNamestringnoMax 120 chars

⚠️ The four destination fields are optional here, and that is a known gap — see the approval step below for why it weakens the second-person rule. Until the API requires them, make them required in whatever client fronts this.

StatusMeaning
400Nothing confirmed and unpaid is available for this partner, the total is below minPayoutAmountSAR, or the userId is not a member of the organizationId being paid
409A supplied commissionIds list did not fully resolve, or another payout claimed part of it in flight. Nothing was paid out

A partly-resolving commissionIds list is refused outright rather than paid in part: at least one row is already claimed, already paid, still an estimate, void, or somebody else's, and paying a subset of what was asked for is worse than paying nothing.

Approve — a second person, always ​

bash
curl -X POST '/api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/payouts/<id>/approve' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <admin-token>' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{ "destinationAccountNumber": "1234567890", "destinationAccountName": "PT Mitra Reseller" }'
FieldTypeRequiredNotes
destinationBankCodestringnoMax 50 chars. Fills a blank only
destinationBankNamestringnoMax 120 chars. Fills a blank only
destinationAccountNumberstringnoMax 50 chars. Fills a blank only
destinationAccountNamestringnoMax 120 chars. Fills a blank only

Every field is optional, so -d '{}' is a valid approval when the requester already supplied the destination.

⛔ The approver must not be the person who requested the payout — 400 if they are. The destination account is typed per payout, not saved and verified, so a second pair of eyes on where the money is going is the only control there is.

A destination supplied here fills in the blanks on the payout. It cannot overwrite a field the requester already filled: sending a different account number, account name, bank code or bank name than the one on file is refused with 400:

POST /api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/payouts/<id>/approve
{ "destinationAccountNumber": "9999999999" }

400 Bad Request
"This payout was requested against a different destinationAccountNumber. An
 approver checks the destination account, and cannot change it — reject the
 payout so it can be requested again with the right account."

⚠️ "Blanks" is the gap. The destination is optional on the request, so a payout can be created with no account at all — and then the approver supplies it and approves it in the same call, with nobody else having seen it. The second-person rule binds who approves, not who chose the account, whenever the requester left it empty. This is a known gap in the API; until it is closed, require the account on the request in whatever client fronts this, so the approver always has something they did not choose to check. Approval is a statement about a destination, and it means nothing if the destination it describes is not the one that was asked for — without this an approver could redirect a reseller's own request to another account and then mark it paid, with no second person ever seeing the change. A destination that is genuinely wrong is a rejection, not an edit; the partner then requests again with the right account.

Approval is also refused with 400 while there is no account number and account name on file: an approval is a statement that the destination was checked, and there is nothing to check without one.

FieldTypeRequiredNotes
referencestringyesThe bank reference for the completed transfer. Max 200 chars
proofFilefilenoThe bank receipt. PDF, JPG or PNG only, max 10 MiB. Multipart only
payoutCurrencyenumnoSAR (default), USD or IDR
payoutAmountnumberconditionalRequired when payoutCurrency is not SAR. On a SAR payout it may be omitted, or echoed back only if it equals totalAmountSAR
payoutRatenumbernoThe FX rate, in the currency's own direction — see below. Omit it and the current published rate is used. On a SAR payout it must be omitted or exactly 1

On the multipart path every value is sent as text; payoutAmount and payoutRate both accept a numeric string (-F 'payoutAmount=1275000'). An empty form field is treated as not sent, not as zero.

Requires the payout to be APPROVED (400 otherwise), and stamps the same instant on every commission row the payout claimed — a paid payout whose rows still read as unpaid would let the same money be claimed twice. 409 if the payout moved while the request was in flight, and 413 if the file exceeds the size limit.

⛔ There is no way to attach a file uploaded somewhere else. The proof is created BY this route, and the field that used to take a file id is gone. That is what makes a receipt unable to be recorded against the wrong payout: there is no id to get wrong. A file uploaded through the generic /storage route cannot be used as a payout proof.

⚠️ A payout can be paid once. Approval moves it out of REQUESTED and paying moves it out of APPROVED, with no route back, so there is no second attempt that could attach a different receipt to the same payout. A payout paid without a proof cannot have one added afterwards.

Read the receipt back with the three proof routes under Inspect a payout.

A transfer made in another currency ​

totalAmountSAR stays the canonical figure — it is what the partner was owed and it never moves. To record what actually left the bank, name the currency and send payoutAmount.

⛔ payoutRate is written the way the admin exchange-rate screen shows that currency, NOT as SAR-per-unit. This is the same convention settlementRate uses on a shortfall settlement, so one rule covers both admin tabs.

CurrencypayoutRate meansTypical value
IDRIDR per ONE SAR4920 at the time of writing
USDSAR per ONE USD~3.75, the peg
SARthe identity caseexactly 1

The direction is fixed by payoutCurrency and is not selectable — the right number under the wrong direction is the same defect, recorded as if it were deliberate. Each row also stores payoutRateDirection saying which way its own number is written, so a row is always readable without guessing from its currency.

The rate is optional — omit it and the published rate is used ​

This is the case the field exists to serve: the amount is the one figure only the bank slip carries, and the rate does not have to be typed a second time.

bash
-d '{ "reference": "SMOKE632-IDR-LIVE-002", "payoutCurrency": "IDR", "payoutAmount": 246000 }'

The server takes the current published rate, records it, and says so under payoutRateSource:

payoutRateSourceMeaning
ADMINpayoutRate came off the request
LIVEDerived from the current published rate. payoutExchangeRateId names the row it was taken from, when one backs it
IDENTITYA SAR payout, rate 1

⚠️ payoutAmount is never derived, and the rate is never derived FROM it. Deriving the rate as totalAmountSAR ÷ payoutAmount would make the reconcile check below pass by construction and catch nothing. Taking it from the exchange-rate table instead keeps the amount and the rate arriving from two independent places, which is what makes their product a real cross-check.

If no rate is published and none is sent, the request is refused rather than recorded at a rate of 1 — an IDR payout at 1 would claim the partner was sent one rupiah per riyal owed.

Two guards, and they catch different things ​

The window is an order-of-magnitude check and always blocks. USD accepts 2–8 and IDR accepts 200–20000 by default, both configurable per environment. It exists because an inverted rate is an ordinary-looking number: a 0.00023529 typed for IDR, or a 0.2667 typed for USD, sits unchallenged inside any single global bound wide enough to be useful.

The reconcile is an accuracy check on top. payoutAmount and payoutRate are converted to SAR through the row's own direction and compared against the SAR owed, with a 1% tolerance for bank rounding. A rate inside its window can still be the wrong rate, and only this catches that:

{ "payoutCurrency": "IDR", "payoutAmount": 246000, "payoutRate": 8000 }

400 Bad Request
"246000 IDR at a rate of 8000 is 30.75 SAR, which does not reconcile with the
 50 SAR owed. payoutRate is IDR per 1 SAR — check it is not inverted."

The window fires first, and on the value this convention exists to catch — the SAR-per-unit spelling that was correct before the rate moved to its canonical direction:

{ "payoutCurrency": "IDR", "payoutAmount": 212500, "payoutRate": 0.00023529 }

400 Bad Request
"Rate 0.00023529 is outside the accepted window for IDR (200 to 20000, read as
 IDR per 1 SAR). Check the rate is not inverted or off by a factor of a
 thousand."

A rate the SERVER derived that lands outside its window is reported differently, because the fix is not to retype it:

"… This rate was taken from the published rate (LIVE), not from the request, so the published rate is unusable. Send payoutRate explicitly to record this payout."

The SAR case ​

A SAR transfer is the identity case: the rate is 1, the amount is the SAR total, and payoutRateSource is IDENTITY rather than ADMIN — the 1 is the trivial case, not a figure anybody asserted. Sending only a reference:

The list answers which payouts exist. This answers what is inside one. For a payout somebody is about to approve or pay, the commission rows it claimed are the thing being approved.

404 when the id does not resolve. Every money field is a decimal string, unpadded — 50, never 50.00.

⚠️ The commission array is capped ​

commissions carries the newest 20 rows only. commissionCount is the true total and commissionLimit is the cap.

Do not infer completeness from the array length. When commissionCount > commissionLimit there are rows you have not been shown. Page the rest with GET /api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions?payoutId=<id>&page=2.

The maker/checker/payer trail is resolved to names ​

requestedBy, approvedBy, rejectedBy and paidBy are user ids, and each has a matching …ByUser object carrying id, name and email. The reason is the two-person rule: approval refuses an approver who requested the payout, and that rule cannot be checked by reading four opaque ids.

The id stays alongside the name. A …ByUser of null against a non-null id means the staff account no longer resolves — the id is still the audit fact.

⚠️ email is nullable on any user object here, partner or staff.

Read the transfer receipt ​

Three routes, all gated on reseller-commissions:view — the same permission as the payout row itself, which already shows the destination bank name, account number and account name, so the receipt discloses nothing the row beside it does not.

bash
# Authenticated download. Always an attachment, never rendered inline.
curl -X GET '/api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/payouts/<id>/proof' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <admin-token>' -OJ
bash
# A no-login link, for an <img src> preview.
curl -X POST '/api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/payouts/<id>/proof/link' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <admin-token>'
# → { "token": "…", "url": "/api/v1/reseller-commissions/payout-proof/<token>",
#     "expiresAt": "…24 hours out…" }
bash
# Revoke every live link for this payout's receipt.
curl -X DELETE '/api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/payouts/<id>/proof/link' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <admin-token>'
# → { "revoked": 2 }

⛔ Anyone holding the minted URL can read the receipt. No login, no permission check, until it expires 24 hours after minting. It exists because an <img> tag cannot send an Authorization header, so the authenticated route renders as a broken image in a preview pane. Treat the URL like the file: a link pasted into a group chat is readable by everyone in that chat for the rest of the day. Revoke it the moment it goes somewhere it should not.

⚠️ A PDF receipt downloads rather than previewing. Only bytes that prove to be a PNG, JPEG or WebP are served inline; everything else, PDF included, is forced to a download. The type is decided by the file bytes: the type recorded at upload is kept only when a signature confirms it, and replaced when the bytes contradict it.

{ "revoked": 0 } is a success, not an error. It also does not mean the file is unreachable — a token minted for the same file through the generic POST /storage/{fileId}/token is a different rail, is not deleted here, and is revoked through DELETE /storage/tokens/{tokenId}.

All three answer 404 for a payout that does not exist, a payout carrying no receipt, and a receipt whose stored file is gone — one answer for all three, so a caller cannot tell which.

6. One partner's totals ​

What an admin needs before deciding whether to start a batch: the same four figures the partner sees on their own summary.

bash
curl -X GET '/api/v1/admin/reseller-commissions/totals?userId=<userId>' \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer <admin-token>'
json
{
  "estimatedSAR": "50",
  "availableSAR": "40",
  "paidSAR": "40",
  "minPayoutAmountSAR": "0"
}
FieldMeaning
estimatedSARAccrued but NOT payable — those bookings have not been closed, and the confirmed figure can come out lower
availableSARConfirmed and unclaimed. This is what can be paid
paidSARAlready transferred
minPayoutAmountSARThe active floor. Shown even when 0, because 0 is the absence of a floor rather than a safe one

⛔ userId alone identifies the partner, and there is no organizationId parameter. The server reads the partner's organization off their user row. This is deliberate: the earner predicate lets an organization id override a user id, so a route accepting both would answer a clean 200 reading zero for an organization partner looked up by user id — a wrong number indistinguishable from a partner who earned nothing.

Pass the same userId that appears on a commission row or a payout, and that POST /payouts takes. 404 when it does not resolve.

⚠️ Unlike the partner's own summary, this does not require the partner to have a mitra registration. An organization that reached the tier by an admin action has no registration row, and this route still reads its totals — that partner can only be paid from this surface, so refusing here would strand them.

Granting the tier ​

Two routes in, both covered elsewhere:

  • Mitra registration. An applicant picks RESELLER on the form and an admin approves it after confirming the refundable security deposit — see Mitra Registrations.
  • A deliberate admin move. PATCH /admin/organizations/:id/pricing-tier or the per-user override — see Organizations and Users.

⚠️ An organization moved to RESELLER by hand has no registered contact account, so nobody at that organization can read its commission or request a payout until it registers as a mitra. Its payouts have to be started from this admin surface.
