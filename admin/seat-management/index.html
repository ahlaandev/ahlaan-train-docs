Seat Management ​

Admin endpoints for viewing a booking's seat map and moving a passenger to another seat, on behalf of a customer.

Endpoints ​

EndpointPermissionDescription
GET /api/v1/admin/ota/bookings/:id/seat-mapbookings:readSeat map for every segment, plus the seats the booking holds
PATCH /api/v1/admin/ota/bookings/:id/seatbookings:modifyMove one passenger to one seat on one segment

Both permissions already exist. Seat handling is treated as a booking modification rather than a capability of its own, so no role needs a new grant.

Unlike the customer routes, these are not scoped to a booking owner - an admin may act on any standard booking. The request and response shapes are the ones in the customer guide; this page covers what differs for an agent.

Which bookings. REGULAR and GROUP bookings, both. A BlockSeat booking answers 404 on both routes - those bookings live on their own routes, and no seat route exists there yet.

What an agent needs to know ​

The carrier's rules, not ours. Each of these has caused a support question:

  1. A booking already has a seat. The carrier assigns one at creation, before payment, and seats a party together. There is never a seatless booking to fix.
  2. The seat map needs a paid booking. Marking an invoice paid on our side is not enough - if the carrier still considers the booking unpaid it will refuse, and the seat map stays unavailable until real payment reaches it.
  3. The window closes 72 hours before departure. A booking made inside that window cannot change seats at all, and no override exists on our side or theirs. Such a booking answers with its own message - that it was made too close to departure - rather than quoting a deadline that fell before it was booked.
  4. Seats may change after ticketing. This surprises agents who know that passenger names cannot.
  5. A GROUP passenger must be named first. The rule is per passenger, so part of a group may be movable while the rest is not.
  6. An infant is a lap passenger. It travels on the accompanying adult's lap with no seat of its own, and shows as passengerType: "INF" in currentSeats carrying the adult's coach and seat - so an infant row looking identical to the adult's is correct, not a duplicate. Asking to move one is refused with 400. Move the adult and the infant goes with them.

When a seat can be moved, and when it cannot ​

Most support questions about seats are really questions about where the booking is in this sequence. The shaded block is the only part in which a seat can move.

Reading this as an agent

  • "The customer cannot see the seat map" is almost always step 1 or the last step: the booking is not paid at the carrier, or the trip is inside 72 hours. Both answer 400 with a message written to be read out to the customer.
  • A ticketed booking can still move a seat. That is the surprise in this diagram - passenger names cannot change after ticketing, seats can.
  • The last step costs nothing upstream. The window is checked against the booking's own stored deadline, so a closed window is refused without asking the carrier.
  • After a move, tell the customer to open their ticket link again. The document is rendered on demand and always carries the current seat; the copy in their inbox does not. Use POST /api/v1/admin/ota/bookings/{id}/resend-ticket-confirmation-email if they need a fresh copy delivered.

Get the seat map ​

http
GET /api/v1/admin/ota/bookings/{bookingId}/seat-map
Authorization: Bearer <admin-jwt>

Returns segments, each with coaches and their seats, plus currentSeats.

currentSeats is the important one for support work: it is the only place that maps a passenger to a segment to a seat. The map marks a seat as taken without naming who holds it, so "which seat is Mr X in" can only be answered from currentSeats.

How to tell a free seat from a taken one, from one this booking holds, and how to lay the grid out, is in the customer guide under Which seats to render how and Laying out the map. The response is the same object on both routes.

GROUP bookings. The map is readable with no passenger named. A change needs the passenger named first, and the seat map does not say who is named - read the booking detail's passengers and its progress.completed. Details in the customer guide under Regular and GROUP bookings.

Change a seat ​

http
PATCH /api/v1/admin/ota/bookings/{bookingId}/seat
Authorization: Bearer <admin-jwt>
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "segmentRph": "1",
  "travelerRph": 1,
  "seatNumber": "150"
}
  • One seat per request. Moving a family means several requests, each of which can fail on its own.
  • Do not send a coach. The carrier resolves it from the seat number, and a move may cross coaches.
  • There is no cost, and no limit on how many times a seat may move inside the window.

After a change: tell the customer to download again ​

The customer's existing ticket link keeps working and always renders the current seat, because the document is generated on demand and the e-ticket number does not change.

A ticket the customer already saved, or received as an email attachment, still shows the old seat. This service sends no email on a seat change. If the customer needs a fresh copy in their inbox, use the existing resend on the booking:

http
POST /api/v1/admin/ota/bookings/{bookingId}/resend-ticket-confirmation-email

Stored seat data ​

After a successful change the booking's stored seat and coach are refreshed from a fresh carrier read, so the booking detail and any manifest reflect the new seat.

Two details worth knowing when investigating a discrepancy:

  • the refresh reads a fresh booking read, never the change response itself. That response carries seat details only for the passenger who moved, so using it would blank the stored seats of everyone else on the booking;
  • the carrier rewrites its internal provider ticket number on every seat change. The e-ticket number, which is what the customer sees, does not change.

Errors ​

StatusMeaning
400Not paid yet, window closed, passenger unnamed, passenger not on the booking, an infant, or the seat number does not exist. Each carries its own message, written to be read to the customer
401No token, or an expired one
403Missing bookings:read or bookings:modify
404Booking not found, or a BlockSeat booking
409The seat was taken between loading the map and submitting
502Carrier refused for a reason we do not recognise. The detail is logged with a correlation id

The messages ​

The 400, 409 and 502 messages, word for word, are in the customer guide under What an error actually looks like. This route sends the same ones.

The one most often asked about is the last-minute booking. That wording exists because such a booking is created with its window already shut, so the deadline falls before the booking did. Quoting it would tell a customer they missed a chance they never had. Nothing was misconfigured and there is no override - the seat the carrier assigned at booking is the seat they travel on.

A refusal raised by our own request checks - a segment or traveller that is not on the booking, a missing seat number - is a 400 carrying that reason verbatim, never a 502. 502 means the carrier answered something the service has no mapping for; the log line holds the carrier's own detail and its correlation id:

[OTA_AirSeatMapRQ] failed {"bookingRef":"NK6GK3","status":400,"code":"448",
 "detail":"SeatMap process failure: [Segment JXD-MKX 2026-09-11] Seat selection is not allowed
 before payment is completed","correlationId":"7a8fdd00-27d2-49a0-a429-795245d2fecf"}

That particular one is mapped, and reaches the caller as 400 Seats can only be chosen once the booking has been paid. The example shows what to look for in the log when an unmapped one appears.

The carrier's code is not a reliable discriminator and neither is its title. Three different codes were captured on the same endpoint within one session - 448 on the payment refusal, 9999 on a seat somebody else holds ("the seat <id> / <from> / <to> is not available"), and 450 on a seat number that does not exist ("Space id not found for seat 9999 on segment 1"). The detail string is the only part that identifies the cause, which is why the mapping matches on it.

Checking a refusal without spending a carrier call ​

The window check runs locally, off the booking's stored time limits, so a closed window is refused before any upstream request. Measured on a booking whose window closed on 15 July:

GET /api/v1/admin/ota/bookings/{id}/seat-map
400 {"message":"Seat changes are no longer possible for this booking.
     The window closed at 15 Jul 2026, 16:30 WIB."}

The deadline is rendered in WIB. Zero OTA_AirSeatMapRQ calls were made for that request.
