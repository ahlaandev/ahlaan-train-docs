Mitra Registration Guide ​

Self-serve onboarding for Mitra (partner) applicants. A mitra confirms their email address with a one-time code, receives their SPK document number, then submits one form — the registration details and the signed MoU together, in a single request. Once an admin approves, they are issued a per-tier registration number (MS. / MG.) and the pricing access for their tier.

Two different numbers, two different moments. The SPK document number (05.100/ADI/07/2026) is yours as soon as you confirm your email — write it on the MoU you sign. The partner number (MS.001 / MG.001) only exists once an admin has approved you.

Mitra vs B2B vs Partner. Mitra is a customer-onboarding flow, distinct from the admin-provisioned banking Partner API-key module and from the B2B KYC flow (which requires NIB/NPWP). Individuals with no company documents can register as mitra.

Quick Overview ​

TierPrefixOn approval
Mitra SilverMS.Promo pricing access
Mitra GoldMG.Promo + special-B2B pricing access

Who can apply: anyone. You do not need an organization.

Flow at a glance ​

Registration is two phases with a review in the middle: you prove your email and get your SPK number, you submit the form and documents, then an admin approves and your partner number appears. Two numbers are issued at two different moments — the SPK document number the instant you verify your email, the partner number (MS./MG.) only on approval.

The registration lifecycle is a small state machine. A rejection is not the end — you keep your login and your SPK number and simply submit again.

Step-by-step workflow ​

The end-to-end walk-through, with what each step changes. Every request is against https://<host>/api/v1; endpoint details are in API Endpoints.

Registration intake can be closed on a given deployment. Phases A and B — requesting a code, verifying it, and submitting — then answer 403 with "Mitra registration is not open at the moment. Please contact Ahlaan to be onboarded." It is a deployment-wide switch, not a judgement about the caller: retrying, signing in, or using a different address changes nothing. Phases C and D are unaffected — an application that already exists can still receive documents, still reports its status, and is still reviewed and approved normally. Handle the 403 by showing the message and hiding the register entry point, not by treating it as a failed request.

Phase A — Prove your email (no authentication)

  1. Request a code — POST /auth/mitra/register/otp/request with { "target": "you@example.com" }. Always 201 {"message":"OTP sent successfully"}, whether or not an account exists — infer nothing from it.
  2. Verify the code — POST /auth/mitra/register/otp/verify with { email, code, name }. On success 201 returns your accessToken (hold it — the next phase needs it) and your documentNumber (e.g. 05.100/ADI/07/2026). Write that number on the SPK/MoU, sign it, scan it. Lost it? GET /auth/mitra/registration/document-number.

Phase B — Submit (Bearer token from step 2 required)

  1. Send the registration — POST /auth/mitra/register as multipart/form-data: the form fields and the signed spk file in one request. The email comes from your token, not the form. 201 returns the registration at status: "PENDING"; the SPK is stored renamed to your document number (05.100_ADI_07_2026.docx). COMPANY without a companyName → 400; no spk → 400; no token → 401.

Phase C — Complete your documents (before approval)

  1. Add the rest — POST /auth/mitra/register/:id/upload/:docType for payment-proof (all tiers), ktp (INDIVIDUAL only), or a replacement an admin asked for. Each returns 201 with the document linked. Approval needs SPK + payment proof (+ KTP for INDIVIDUAL, + the 50,000 SAR deposit proof for GOLD). The partnership file is not required for approval.

Phase D — Review

  1. Track it — GET /auth/mitra/application/status shows PENDING (partner number still null). On the mitra portal GET /auth/profile reports promoAccessLevel: APPLICANT, /wallet is 403, and public search still works. On the main site you remain an ordinary customer with a working wallet.
  2. Admin decides. Approve mints your per-tier number — MG.001 (Gold) / MS.001 (Silver) — from separate counters. What else it does depends on your mode: NEW_ORG creates an Organization + SAR org wallet and promotes you to B2B; ORG_LESS (individual) stays B2C with the tier flags on your own account; MEMBER grants your existing organization the tier in place (no new org, no wallet, no role change). A GOLD approval in any mode is blocked until the admin confirms the 50,000 SAR deposit — approving without it returns 400 "Confirm the 50,000 SAR GOLD deposit (received off-system) before approval". Reject sets a rejectionReason you can read.

Phase E — After approval (no re-login)

  1. Your existing session becomes a partner session. application/status now carries registrationNumber; GET /auth/profile reports promoAccessLevel: PARTNER; the wallet and partner endpoints open on the mitra portal. Rejected instead? Read rejectionReason and loop back to step 3 — same token, same SPK number, brand-new registration id.

Registration modes — the server decides, not you ​

The server derives one of three modes from the account behind your Bearer token (never from a body flag), and returns it as mode on every registration object:

Your accountmodeWhat the org form doesOn approval
A B2B member of an organizationMEMBERIgnored — any companyName/companyAddress/companyPhone/nib/npwp you send is dropped, not stored, not validated. No company email is collected.Your existing organization gains mitra access (SILVER/GOLD tier + promo). No new org, no wallet, no role change.
Not a member, you send companyNameNEW_ORGRequired — companyAddress, companyPhone, nib, npwp must accompany companyName.A new Organization + SAR wallet is created; your account becomes a B2B member of it.
Not a member, no companyNameORG_LESSNone.No organization. You are an individual partner: your account stays B2C and the tier + promo flags are set on you. Pricing is identical to an organization of the same tier.

A B2B member of an organization may opt out of MEMBER mode and register as an individual by sending registerAsIndividual: true — that forces ORG_LESS, and only at admin approval is your account detached from its organization (role → B2C, org link cleared).

An admin account cannot register as a mitra (409).

For an existing organization member (MEMBER) ​

If you already belong to an organization, you may register to switch that organization on to mitra pricing for the first time. Pick SILVER or GOLD as its first tier — approval flips the org's promo + tier flags in place. This grants mitra pricing to every member of the org whose personal pricing is inherited from the org. Notes:

  • Your organization's details (name, address, email) are not editable through this form — org edits belong to the organization-management surface. Anything you type in the org fields is ignored.
  • If your organization already holds an approved, numbered mitra registration, registering again is rejected (409). A grandfathered org that only carries the promo-access flag but was never issued a mitra number is not blocked and may register. Later tier changes (Silver ↔ Gold) stay admin-only.
  • GOLD still requires the 50,000 SAR deposit proof + the admin's deposit confirmation, exactly as a new organization does.

For a new organization (NEW_ORG) — forced company email ​

When a new organization is created, its email is always your OTP-verified account email. There is no companyEmail field in the request body — an email in the body could be a stranger's. Because it equals your account email (already unique per account), there is nothing extra to prove.

nib and npwp must be unique across both the mitra flow and the B2B application flow — a value already used by either is rejected with 409.

Blank and whitespace-only values count as not supplied — an empty text input is the same as omitting the field.

Two rules follow from this:

  • applicantType: COMPANY requires companyName. Declaring yourself a company and then not naming one is rejected with 400.
  • applicantType: INDIVIDUAL may still supply a companyName (a sole proprietor who wants an organization). The two fields are independent: applicantType decides whether a KTP is required; companyName decides whether an Organization is created.

Max upload size: 10 MB per file, and 28 MB for all documents together in one registration request.

NEW_ORG must attach the nibDocument and npwpDocument proof files inline, in the same request as the SPK — exactly like the b2b KYC documents. They accept common images (JPEG/PNG/WebP) or PDF and are required at submit for every NEW_ORG registration: both a COMPANY and an INDIVIDUAL sole-proprietor who supplies a companyName. A MEMBER or ORG_LESS submission never sends them. There is no separate later-upload step for the first submission — a NEW_ORG request missing either proof is 400.

The signed MoU (spk) must be attached to the registration itself — it is sent in the same request as the form fields, and a submission without it is rejected. The other documents are optional at submit time and can be added afterwards, once you are signed in (see §2). That is deliberate: you usually will not have a payment proof until you have the bank details.

Approval requires SPK and payment proof (plus KTP for INDIVIDUAL). Approving an INDIVIDUAL with no KTP on file returns 400 "Cannot approve: missing required document(s): KTP" (captured live). The partnership file is deprecated — it is still uploadable and kept for existing records, but it no longer blocks approval. Submitting with only the SPK is fine; being approved with only the SPK is not.

The Gold 50,000 SAR deposit proof is captured here (uploaded as the payment proof). The deposit transfer itself is verified manually by an admin off-system and is not credited to your wallet balance.

API Endpoints ​

Base URL ​

https://<host>/api/v1

The flow, end to end ​

Registration begins by proving your email. Nothing is bound to an address until a code sent to it comes back.

1. POST /auth/mitra/register/otp/request   { target: email }        → "OTP sent"
2. POST /auth/mitra/register/otp/verify    { email, code, name }    → tokens + documentNumber
3. write the documentNumber on your SPK, sign it, scan it
   (lost the number? GET /auth/mitra/registration/document-number)
4. POST /auth/mitra/register  (Bearer)  multipart, with the signed SPK attached
5. GET  /auth/mitra/application/status  (Bearer)                    → PENDING …
6. admin approves → your partner number (MS.001 / MG.001) appears on that status

1. Request an email code ​

Endpoint: POST /auth/mitra/register/otp/request

Authentication: none.

FieldRequiredNotes
targetyesthe email address to register with. It is named target, not email.
bash
curl -X POST "$BASE/auth/mitra/register/otp/request" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"target":"budi@example.com"}'

Response: 201 — {"message": "OTP sent successfully"}.

That response is identical for every input — an address with an account, an address without one, or an address we refuse. It tells you nothing about whether an account exists, and you must not infer anything from it.

Things worth knowing before you build against this:

  • Asking again re-sends the same code; it does not issue a new one (unless the current one has under 60 seconds left, in which case a fresh one is minted). A "resend" button is therefore always safe: it can never invalidate a code the user is already typing — not even if a stranger requests a code for their address. Expect a resend to return a code the user already has. That is correct behaviour, not a bug.
  • 429 can come back from the OTP rate limit (per IP + address; it announces 10 per 5 minutes but blocks on the 10th, so plan for 9). Show the message and let them retry. A 429 does not mean the code was sent.
  • 503 while the service's abuse controls are unavailable. Retry shortly.
  • 403 when self-serve registration is closed on this deployment. This is not about the caller — every address gets it, and no code is sent. Show the message and stop the funnel here; do not offer a retry, and prefer hiding the register entry point outright.

2. Verify the code — get your session and your SPK number ​

Endpoint: POST /auth/mitra/register/otp/verify

Authentication: none (the code is the proof).

FieldRequiredNotes
emailyesthe address the code was sent to
codeyesthe 6 digits from the email
namenoused only if this email has no account yet. An existing account keeps the name it already has. Omitted → the part of the email before the @.
bash
curl -X POST "$BASE/auth/mitra/register/otp/verify" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"email":"budi@example.com","code":"123456","name":"Budi Santoso"}'

Response: 201

json
{
  "accessToken": "eyJ…",
  "refreshToken": "…",
  "user": { "id": "…", "email": "budi@example.com", "name": "Budi Santoso", "role": "B2C", "isEmailConfirmed": true, "isPhoneConfirmed": false },
  "documentNumber": "05.100/ADI/07/2026",
  "organization": null,
  "promoAccessLevel": "DENIED"
}

A first-time holder verifies before submitting a registration, so at this moment they have none on file and promoAccessLevel is DENIED — it climbs to APPLICANT the instant you submit step 4, and to PARTNER once an admin approves it. A returning applicant with a registration already under review therefore verifies straight to APPLICANT, and an already-approved partner re-verifying sees PARTNER with their organization filled in:

json
{
  "...": "…",
  "organization": { "id": "0197f0b2-…", "name": "PT Berkah Travel", "code": "BRK" },
  "promoAccessLevel": "PARTNER"
}

Two things happen here, and both matter:

  1. You get an account and a session. If the email already had a customer account, that account is reused — your name, your bookings, your wallet are all untouched. If it had none, a passwordless one is created. Hold the accessToken: step 4 requires it.
  2. You get your SPK document number. Write this number on the SPK you sign, then upload that signed document in step 4. The number is yours and it is stable — verifying again, or refreshing the page, returns the same one, forever. The server assigns it; you cannot choose it.

The response also carries two routing fields:

  • organization — the org this account belongs to ({ id, name, code }), or null for the org-less holder that is the common case. It is always present, never omitted; only the safe identity triple is surfaced, never the org's pricing/promo settings.
  • promoAccessLevel — the same routing signal GET /auth/profile returns on the mitra host, evaluated at this instant: DENIED for a fresh holder with no registration yet, APPLICANT once one is under review, PARTNER once approved. It is present only on the mitra host and omitted entirely off it.

Errors:

CodeWhen
401Wrong or expired code.
403The account is disabled.
403Self-serve registration is closed on this deployment — a different cause sharing the same status. Tell them apart by the message: "Mitra registration is not open at the moment…" is the closed door, and it applies to everyone rather than to this account. No holder account is created and no SPK number is burnt.
409The email belongs to an admin account, or to a B2B account with no organization (a data anomaly — resolve the account first). A B2B member with an organization is now allowed — it registers in MEMBER mode.
429Too many attempts from this IP.
503The service's abuse controls are unavailable. Retry shortly.

3. Read your SPK number back ​

Endpoint: GET /auth/mitra/registration/document-number

Authentication: Bearer JWT (the one from step 2).

For a page refresh between verifying and submitting.

bash
curl "$BASE/auth/mitra/registration/document-number" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN"

Response: 200 — {"documentNumber": "05.100/ADI/07/2026", "reservedAt": "2026-07-15T00:29:50.875Z"}

It returns the number you already have; it never creates a new one. 404 if you have not verified an email yet.

Recovering the number after an accidental refresh. The SPK number is server-minted and reserved on your account at verification (reservedAt), never held only in the browser — so a refresh cannot lose it. There are two recovery paths depending on whether your session survived:

  • Session kept (the accessToken is persisted, not just page state) — call this endpoint. Same number, every time.
  • Session lost too (hard refresh, cleared storage, or a different device) — just re-run steps 1–2. otp/request re-sends the same code, and otp/verify returns the same documentNumber — verification is idempotent because the reservation is keyed to your email, once and for all.

Either way you get 05.100/ADI/07/2026 back; you can never mint a duplicate or be locked out. The same guarantee is why a rejected applicant keeps their number when they re-apply.

4. Submit a registration (documents included) ​

Endpoint: POST /auth/mitra/register

Content-Type: multipart/form-data — the form fields and the documents travel in one request. There is no separate upload step for the first submission.

Authentication: Bearer JWT (required). Use the token from step 2. An anonymous submission is 401.

The registration takes your email from your token, not from the form. That is why there is no applicantEmail field any more: an email typed into a form proves nothing, and an address you do not control must never be able to pull your registration onto someone else's account.

⚠️ Sign in with the email you registered with. Your account is never matched or created from applicantPhone; the phone is contact data, not a credential. If you already have a customer account under a different email (e.g. a personal one) and you register with your company email, you now have two separate accounts — and only the company one holds the application.

Form fields:

The server takes your email from your token, mints the SPK number itself (step 2), and — for a new organization — uses your account email as the org email. Sending applicantEmail/spkNumber/companyEmail anyway is not an error; they are silently ignored.

If you are an existing organization member, every org field above is ignored — the registration only grants your existing organization mitra access. nib/npwp supplied by a member are dropped (never stored, never dup-checked).

Scalar vs file — two different parts, two different names. nib / npwp carry the numbers (text); nibDocument / npwpDocument carry the proof files (image or PDF). They are deliberately named apart so one multipart body can send both — do not reuse the nib / npwp name for the file.

A NEW_ORG submission (non-member with a companyName) requires all four org fields — companyAddress, companyPhone, nib, npwp — alongside companyName, and the nibDocument + npwpDocument proof files (image or PDF) attached inline. (listing whichever are missing — the scalar fields by name, the missing proof files as nib document / npwp document).

If a document fails to store, no registration is created at all and you can simply submit the form again. Nothing is half-created and you are never locked out of retrying.

5. Add or replace a document after signing in ​

Endpoint: POST /auth/mitra/register/:id/upload/:docType

Authentication: Bearer JWT (required). You must own the registration. Because every registration is bound to an account at submit time, an unauthenticated call here is always 403 — the first submission attaches its documents inline (§4) instead.

Use this to:

  • add the payment proof once you have the bank details;
  • replace a document an admin asked you to redo.

docType ∈ { spk, ktp, payment-proof, nibDocument, npwpDocument }. Multipart form field file. (nibDocument/npwpDocument are accepted here too, so a NEW_ORG applicant can replace a proof an admin asked them to redo; they accept images (JPEG/PNG/WebP) or PDF.) partnership is deprecated — the endpoint still accepts it for backward compatibility, but new clients must not send it.

bash
curl -X POST "$BASE/auth/mitra/register/$ID/upload/payment-proof" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  -F "file=@proof.jpg"

Response: 201 Created — the updated registration with the document linked, in the customer shape.

Errors: 403 if the registration belongs to another account (or you are not signed in). 400 if the registration is already APPROVED.

A re-uploaded SPK is renamed to your document number (e.g. 05.100_ADI_07_2026.pdf). The registration always carries the number now, so this rename always happens.

6. Check registration status ​

Endpoint: GET /auth/mitra/application/status

Authentication: Bearer JWT (the applicant's account).

Response: 200 OK — your latest registration, in the customer shape. If you were rejected and re-applied, this is the newest application.

While your application is under review ​

You can log in from the moment you submit — with the email you registered with, by OTP or Google — and you stay logged in through a rejection, so you can read why and re-apply.

On the mitra portal, an application that is not yet approved gets you your own application and nothing else:

You canYou cannot (until approved)
GET /auth/profileBook (/custom-booking, /custom-ota/book/calculate)
GET /auth/mitra/application/statusGET /wallet — 403
Re-submit: POST /auth/mitra/register (documents inline)GET /invoice
Add / replace a document: POST /auth/mitra/register/:id/upload/:docType
GET /storage/{id}/download — your own documents
Log out

Everything else on the mitra portal returns 403.

Two things that surprise people, both deliberate:

  • Public search still works. POST /custom-ota/search is a guest surface, so you get the same public result (and the same public tier prices) a logged-out visitor gets. Logging in does not unlock partner pricing — approval does.
  • On the main site nothing changes. There you are an ordinary customer, with a working wallet, for as long as your application is pending.

GET /auth/profile tells the frontend which of these you are, on the mitra portal only:

json
{ "promoAccessLevel": "APPLICANT" }

APPLICANT ⇒ your application is in flight. PARTNER ⇒ you are approved and the full portal is open and hold a numbered registration — promoAccessEnabled alone is no longer sufficient. DENIED ⇒ this account has no access to the mitra portal.

When you hit a partner route (e.g. GET /v1/wallet) while confined, the 403 body is tailored to your level — the frontend can show it verbatim (captured live on mitra.ahlaan.co.id):

json
// APPLICANT
{ "message": "Your mitra application is still under review.", "error": "Forbidden", "statusCode": 403 }
// REGISTRATION_REQUIRED
{ "message": "Complete your mitra registration to access partner features.", "error": "Forbidden", "statusCode": 403 }
// DENIED
{ "message": "This portal is available only for partners.", "error": "Forbidden", "statusCode": 403 }

REGISTRATION_REQUIRED ⇒ a grandfathered promo account that has no numbered mitra registration (promo access was granted outside the mitra flow). You may log in, but you are hard-locked to the register form exactly like an APPLICANT — no partner prices or pages — until you (re-)register and an admin approves you.

The moment an admin approves you, your existing session becomes a partner session — no re-login.

If you are rejected ​

You keep your login. Read rejectionReason, then submit a new registration (POST /auth/mitra/register again, with your documents attached as before, and with the Bearer token you already hold) — you get a brand-new registration id carrying them. application/status then shows the new application. Your old rejected one stays on file.

You do not need to redo the email step, and your SPK document number does not change — it is bound to your email address, once and for all.

Post-Approval ​

Both kinds of partner receive:

  • a per-tier registration number (MS.001 … MS.999 … MS.1000, likewise MG.) — drawn from one shared per-tier counter, so organization and individual partners are numbered in the same sequence;
  • the tier's pricing access (Gold ⇒ special + promo; Silver ⇒ promo);
  • an approval email.

What differs:

Organization partnerIndividual partner
Organizationcreatednone
Organization walletcreated (SAR, balance 0)none
Account rolepromoted to B2Bstays B2C
Where the tier liveson the organizationon your account
Personal SAR walletyes (created at registration)yes (created at registration)

Your personal SAR wallet is created when you register, not at approval. The wallet endpoints work from submission onward on the main site — but they are 403 on the mitra portal until you are approved (see While your application is under review).

A Gold individual partner still owes the 50,000 SAR deposit. The deposit is keyed on the tier, not on whether you have a company. It is verified manually by an admin off-system and is not credited to any wallet balance.

One approved registration per account, ever. After approval you cannot submit another registration (409) — including to change tier. Contact an admin instead.

Impersonation ("Act as customer") — allowed URLs ​

An admin can open an "Act as customer" (impersonation) session to complete a custom booking on a partner's behalf. That session carries a confined token that is valid only on the read/checkout surface below — every credential-mutating and money-moving route stays blocked. The canonical list lives in the admin doc (Impersonation); it is mirrored here for partner-facing reference and both are kept in lockstep with the IMPERSONATION_ALLOWED_PATH_PATTERNS constant (the constant is authoritative if they ever disagree).

Allowed while impersonating:

  • …/custom-ota/* — search, availability, calculate
  • …/custom-booking/* — create, manage, cancel, detail
  • …/invoice/:id/generate-payment, …/invoice/:id/available-gateways, …/invoice/:id/payment-options
  • GET …/auth/profile — read only, the exact path (surfaces partner.registrationNumber + pricingTier); the …/auth/profile/* subtree stays blocked (credential/contact-person writes)
  • GET …/invoice/:id, GET …/invoice/:id/download, GET …/invoice/:id/payments — the invoice being paid, its PDF data, and its payment attempts (read-only)
  • the …/payments/* subtree — today the only route here is manual-transfer: GET …/payments/:paymentId/manual-transfer (read the instruction) and POST …/payments/:paymentId/manual-transfer/proof (upload the transfer proof), both owner-scoped to the impersonated customer's own payment
  • GET …/exchange-rate/current, GET …/exchange-rate/history — read-only rate reads, scoped to the impersonated partner's own tier (gold/silver) so the rate widget shows the partner's tier rate during impersonation instead of the guest/holder peg

Blocked on purpose: the invoice list, …/wallet/*, bank-details, every profile/identifier write, the singular …/payment/* and …/payment-gateway/* roots, and every /admin/* route.
