Seat Selection ​

This guide explains how a passenger views the seat map for a booking and moves to a different seat.

Quick Overview ​

FeatureDescription
Seat MapGET /api/v1/ota/bookings/:id/seat-map - Every seat on the train, plus the seats this booking holds
Change SeatPATCH /api/v1/ota/bookings/:id/seat - Move one passenger to one seat on one segment
AuthenticationRequired. Both endpoints are scoped to the booking owner
Booking kindsREGULAR and GROUP bookings on the ota/* routes. BlockSeat bookings are out of scope - see Regular and GROUP bookings
CostNone. The carrier returns no price for a seat and charges nothing for a change
Rate limitSeat map 20 requests per minute, seat change 10 per minute, per IP. Over the limit answers 429

The three rules that decide everything ​

Before building a screen against these endpoints, read this section. Most support questions come from here.

1. Every booking already has a seat ​

The carrier assigns a seat when the booking is created, before any payment, and keeps a party together where it can. A three passenger booking was assigned 137, 138 and 139 on one measurement.

So this feature is change your seat, not choose a seat. The assigned coach and seat are available on the booking detail from the moment the booking exists, and can be shown on a confirmation screen before payment.

2. The seat map only exists after payment ​

The carrier will not release a seat map for an unpaid booking, and there is no seat map for a train in general. It exists only in the context of one paid booking. A request before payment is refused with 400.

There are two refusals here, and they read differently. The service checks the booking's own status first and answers without calling the carrier at all. If that check passes but the carrier still does not consider the booking paid - which happens when an invoice was settled on our side before the money reached the carrier - the carrier refuses instead, and that refusal is translated into our own wording. Both are 400; only the second costs an upstream call.

This means a seat picker cannot be offered during checkout, before the booking is paid for. Place it after payment, or on the booking detail page.

3. The window closes 72 hours before departure ​

The carrier sets a change deadline at exactly 72 hours before the outbound departure. After that, neither the map nor a change is possible.

A booking made inside 72 hours of departure therefore has no window at all - it is already closed when the booking is created. This is a normal, everyday situation for a last minute traveller, not an error. Show the seat section with an explanation rather than hiding it or letting the request fail.

That case gets its own message, because the deadline fell before the booking existed and quoting it would read as though the passenger missed something they could have done:

This booking was made too close to departure, so seats were already closed when it was created. The seat assigned at booking is the one you travel on.

A booking whose window closed after it was made still gets the deadline, which can be shown as-is:

Seat changes are no longer possible for this booking. The window closed at 15 Jul 2026, 16:30 WIB.

Where a seat change sits in the whole journey ​

The seat screen is reachable only in one window of the booking's life. This is the journey end to end, so a client knows what must already have happened before it renders anything.

What a client must take from this

  • Steps 3 and 4 are impossible before step 2 finishes. Not "empty", not "retryable" - refused with 400. The booking carries a seat from step 1, so a pre-payment screen can show the seat it already has; it cannot show the map or offer a change.
  • The window is measured from departure, not from payment. A booking made inside 72 hours of departure goes from step 2 to step 5 with no step 3 or 4 ever available.
  • Step 4 moves one passenger. Two passengers is two requests, each able to fail on its own.
  • Step 5 is the passenger's existing link. Nothing new is issued: the document is rendered on demand and always carries the current seat. A PDF already saved or emailed keeps the old seat forever, which is why the step 4 response says so and the client should show that message rather than a generic success toast.

How much of this is proven. All five steps were exercised end to end on staging against the live carrier, on three bookings paid through the Xendit sandbox: a one-way, a round trip, and a GROUP booking of 11 passengers. The seat map returned real coaches and seats, a change moved the passenger, the stored seat was refreshed, and the re-downloaded PDF printed the new seat while the old one appeared nowhere in it. On the round trip a change to the return leg left the outbound seat untouched. On the GROUP booking the passenger that moved had been named first; an unnamed one is refused, see Regular and GROUP bookings.

One case this run did not cover: a seat change on an infant, which is refused before any carrier call is made.

Get the seat map ​

http
GET /api/v1/ota/bookings/{bookingId}/seat-map
Authorization: Bearer <token>

Response ​

The carrier returns every seat in every coach of the train, occupied ones included, and so does this response: a coach's seats array holds totalSeatQty entries. The example below is cut down to stay readable. In a real response:

  • segments has one entry per leg, so two on a round trip;
  • coaches has one entry per coach on the train, not one - a 26-coach service was measured;
  • seats has every seat in that coach, here cut to one free and one taken;
  • currentSeats has one entry per passenger per segment, here a one-passenger one-way booking.

Reading the response ​

  • segments carries one entry per leg. A round trip returns two, segmentRph "1" for the outbound and "2" for the return.
  • changeDeadline is optional. It is absent when the carrier sent no deadline for the booking, and a request on a booking whose deadline has passed is refused with 400 before this response is built. Treat a missing value as "unknown", not as "no deadline".
  • currentSeats is the only place that says which passenger holds which seat, and carries each one's passengerType. The map itself marks a seat as taken without naming the holder, so this list cannot be derived from segments. seat and coach are null when the carrier's coupon carries no seat for that passenger and segment.
  • tripClass is the coach's class as the API's TripClass value, ECONOMY or BUSINESS, not the carrier's cabin code. It is absent when the carrier sends a cabin code other than its documented P and T. cabinType is that raw code, kept for troubleshooting.
  • passengerType on currentSeats is the carrier's passenger type code, passed through as a plain string, in the same ADT / CHD / INF values as the booking's passengers.

Which seats to render how ​

Each seat carries three fields about its state, and one rule for reading them:

Render asRuleNotes
Selectableavailable: trueThe one state a passenger may pick. Equivalent to availability: "SEAT_AVAILABLE"
Held by this bookingoccupiedByThisBooking: trueOne of this booking's own passengers sits here. Not selectable. Read currentSeats for who
Not selectableboth falseTaken by someone else, or not for sale

available is true for exactly one carrier value and false for everything else, including values the carrier's own documentation does not list. Drive selectability from it alone.

availability is the carrier's raw value, kept so a client can tell the two "not selectable" cases apart if it wants to render them differently:

availabilityMeaning
SEAT_AVAILABLEFree
SEAT_OCCUPIEDHeld by another booking
SEAT_RESERVEDHeld by this booking
SEAT_BLOCKED_OTHER_REASONNot for sale. Returned by the carrier, absent from its documentation

Do not derive selectability from this string, and do not assume the list above is complete: a value not in it must render as not selectable, which is what available: false already gives you.

occupiedByThisBooking is true when the carrier reports SEAT_RESERVED, or when the seat's coach and number match one of currentSeats for that segment. Seat numbers repeat across coaches on one train, so a seat is identified by coach plus number, never number alone.

Laying out the map ​

The carrier returns a seat grid per coach, and the response keeps the two coordinates a grid needs:

  • one block per coach, in the order the carrier lists them, with code as the coach label and totalSeatQty as its seat count;
  • inside a coach, rowNumber and columnNumber place each seat. Draw rows top to bottom and columns left to right; a column number missing from a row is a gap, normally the aisle. Column numbering starts at 0: the carrier's own documentation sample has rows carrying columns 0, 1, 3 and 4, with 2 absent.

The carrier's overall grid dimensions (its columnSpan, startingRow and endingRow) are not forwarded, so size the grid from the largest rowNumber and columnNumber present in that coach. rowNumber and columnNumber are optional in the schema; a seat missing either cannot be placed and should be listed rather than drawn.

features describes the seat, one entry per feature. The values are the carrier's own, passed through as plain strings: the API schema lists the documented ones below, and a value outside them means the carrier added one, so treat it as unknown rather than an error.

valueextensionMeaning
WINDOW-Window seat
AISLE-Aisle seat
OTHERForwardFacing or BackwardFacingWhich way the seat faces. Train seats face both ways, so show it

Size ​

The map is large. A single segment is a few hundred kilobytes at the carrier before it is reduced, and a round trip roughly doubles that. Fetch it when the passenger opens the seat screen rather than eagerly, and do not cache it: a seat taken between render and submit is the case the 409 below exists for.

Change a seat ​

http
PATCH /api/v1/ota/bookings/{bookingId}/seat
Authorization: Bearer <token>
Content-Type: application/json

{
  "segmentRph": "1",
  "travelerRph": 1,
  "seatNumber": "150"
}

Rules ​

  • One seat per request. To move several passengers, send several requests and handle each result separately. There is no batch form.
  • Do not send a coach. The carrier works it out from the seat number, and a move may cross coaches.
  • There is no limit on how many times a seat may change while the window is open.
  • A ticketed booking may still change seats. This is unlike a passenger name, which the carrier refuses to change once tickets are issued.

Response ​

currentSeats here lists every passenger on the booking, per segment, not only the one that moved: it comes from a fresh read of the whole booking. The example is a one-passenger one-way booking.

json
{
  "success": true,
  "message": "Seat updated to 150 in coach 006. Open your ticket link again to download the updated ticket - a ticket already saved or emailed still shows the old seat.",
  "currentSeats": [
    {
      "travelerRph": 1,
      "segmentRph": "1",
      "seat": "150",
      "coach": "006",
      "passengerType": "ADT"
    }
  ]
}

Tickets after a seat change ​

The download link the passenger already has keeps working and always shows the current seat - the ticket document is generated fresh each time it is opened, and the e-ticket number never changes.

What does not update is a copy already delivered: a PDF saved to a phone, or the attachment on a confirmation email, was rendered before the change and keeps the old seat forever. This service sends no email on a seat change. That is why the response message tells the passenger to download again, and the client should surface that message rather than replacing it with a generic success toast.

Regular and GROUP bookings ​

Both endpoints serve REGULAR and GROUP bookings, on the same paths and with the same request and response shapes. The differences are in what a GROUP booking is:

REGULARGROUP
PassengersNamed at bookingCreated from counts; names added later, see Update GROUP Booking Passengers
Reading the mapAfter payment, inside the windowSame. No name is needed to read the map
Changing a seatAfter payment, inside the windowSame, plus the passenger must already be named. The rule is per passenger: a named one can move while unnamed ones on the same booking cannot

The seat map does not say whether a passenger is named. currentSeats carries no name. To enable the seat control per passenger on a GROUP booking, read the booking detail: its passengers[].rph matches travelerRph here, and a passenger not yet counted in its progress.completed is not movable yet. The refusal for an unnamed passenger comes either from this service or from the carrier; both are 400 with the same message.

BlockSeat bookings are out of scope. The wholesale BlockSeat flow keeps its bookings on separate routes, and the seat routes above only look at standard bookings, so a BlockSeat booking id answers 404 here. There is no seat endpoint on the BlockSeat routes.

Passengers who cannot be moved ​

PassengerBehaviour
InfantTravels on the accompanying adult's lap and is not allocated a seat of its own - it shares the adult's. currentSeats[].passengerType is INF for one, and it reports the adult's coach and seat, which is why an infant row looks like a duplicate of the adult's. Do not render a seat control for that row. Asking to move one is refused with 400. Moving the adult moves the infant with it
Unnamed GROUP passengerSee Regular and GROUP bookings

What an error actually looks like ​

Every error carries the service's standard envelope, not a bare message:

json
{
  "statusCode": 400,
  "error": "Bad Request",
  "message": "Seats can only be chosen once the booking has been paid.",
  "code": "UNCODED",
  "category": "VALIDATION",
  "retryable": false
}

Most of these can be shown to a passenger as they are, but not all of them. A few need handling by the client rather than display:

  • the "not paid on our side" one ends in Current status: PENDING, and that word is the internal booking status - it can also read FAILED;
  • the "not on this booking" and "not part of this booking" ones are client-side mistakes, not something a passenger can act on;
  • a validation failure returns an array in message, not a string;
  • the deadline in the locally checked window message is rendered in WIB.
